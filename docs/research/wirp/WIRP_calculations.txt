Calculations
This document provides examples of how WIRP leverages futures and OIS asset prices to calculate the
number of hikes and cuts expected by a Central Bank. You will also learn the assumptions WIRP relies on
to make calculations, how WIRP generates forward rates (including the data used), and how WIRP uses
these forward rates to calculate the implied data displayed in WIRP.

Model Assumptions
Both the Futures and the OIS model rely on the assumption that only a central bank action will impact
the effective interest rate of an economy. This assumption allows the expected overnight rates to be
pushed forward and backward through the chosen asset’s tenor structure, as shown in the following
examples.




Futures Model
To perform the calculations for this model, WIRP collects price data for a list of assets from the Futures
market and uses this data to derive interest rate projections for each upcoming CB meeting. WIRP
assumes that for the current asset prices to exist the rate projections will hold true, as shown in greater
detail in the following example.

Index Swaps (OIS) to imply the rates surrounding CB meeting dates.

Calculating the Average Expected Rate before a Meeting

market, both before and after the meeting of the Federal Reserve.

entered into and the first date of the floating side of the equation or fixing. While spot lag varies from
market to market, it is generally regarded as two days in the OIS market. This is the convention followed
in WIRP. If this lag lands the start of the contract on a weekend, accrual begins on the following business
day. These dates can be referenced on the Bloomberg Swap Manager at SWPM –OIS R09/13/2018
<GO>.

Implied Data
The implied data in WIRP is the data displayed in the data grid and Current Implied O/N Rate data
directly above the grid. The implied data consists of five related data items, three interest rate
expressions and two estimates of CB movements based on those expressions.

Interest Rate Expressions
In the previous sections, we saw how one can estimate forward rates expected before and after a
meeting that would solve out to the individual rates surrounding CB meetings. In the interest rate
expressions, we are simply labeling the data in a slightly different manner.

Current Implied Overnight Rate
The forward rate that is estimated before the first (upcoming) meeting is labeled as the ‘Current Implied
Overnight Rate.’ This interest rate becomes the starting point for our other calculations.

Note: The generic tickers for these data do not have meeting-specific counterparts, as there is only one
for each model.

Post-Meeting Implied Overnight Rate
The forward rates that are estimated for each CB meeting are labeled as the ‘Post-Meeting Implied
Overnight Rates.’ These are the overnight rates which are expected to prevail after each corresponding
CB meeting. A generic ticker references the front meeting, while all other meeting-specific data are
referenced via a virtual tail in the MMMYYYY form (e.g., US0AFR DEC2019 Index).
Implied Overnight Rate Change
The spread between the Current Implied Overnight Rate and the Post-Meeting Implied Overnight Rate.
These indicate the total change in the overnight rate expected from today until the conclusion of the
corresponding CB meeting.

Percent of a Single Hike or Cut
The marginal percent of a single hike or a cut priced in at a specific CB meeting. This number is not
cumulative. Rather than always referencing the Current Implied Overnight Rate, this number looks at
the spread of the implied overnight rates immediately preceding and following a specific central bank
meeting, expressed as a percentage of a single CB move.

The following example from the US Futures model will illustrate.

On September 13th 2018, the US Federal Reserve was expected by markets to hike rates at its upcoming
meeting on the 26th. However, even though markets had priced in 3.5 more hikes through the end of the
next year, the percent of a single hike or cut for the November meeting only had 1.4% of a hike priced
in. Looking back on how the hikes were distributed on that day illustrates why. We can see by looking at
the Implied Rate column that markets were expecting a hike to take rates from ~1.918 to ~2.165 on the
26th. However, markets were only implying a rate of ~2.168 after the November meeting. This is
because, at the time, a dogma persisted that the Fed would only move rates at every other (‘big’)
meeting. Therefore, the percent of a single hike priced for the 11/8/2018 meeting was very small and
would have been calculated as:


                                    2.168 − 2.165
